# At Two Heights

*A city makes places for people. Where does the person go when the city becomes a picture?*

Photographs by Anni McHenry · San Francisco · 20 September 2025

![Looking down at red shoes and strawberry-patterned striped trousers beside a thick yellow curb.](https://fieldlight.com/writing/at-two-heights/assets/curb-1600.webp)

*01 / At the feet — A line beside a body.*

![An aerial night view of an intersection: yellow curves, striped crosswalks, parked cars, and a magenta-lit corner building.](https://fieldlight.com/writing/at-two-heights/assets/intersection-1600.webp)

*02 / Across the streets — Lines made for crossing.*

## I. The line

At the curb, yellow is almost a wall. It runs beside red shoes, wide enough to interrupt the whole photograph. Later, from above, yellow curls through an intersection. Crosswalks fan out. The streets meet in a bright, improbable piece of geometry.

The difference is not that the city has become orderly. The first picture already has its lines. What changes is their relation to a body: *beside me* becomes *around there*. A boundary becomes a composition without ceasing to be a boundary.

## II. The vacancy

### The empty place already has *a shape.*

The boats make a pattern. Their absences make one too. An empty slip keeps the same small architecture as the occupied slip beside it. Before a boat arrives, a place has been made for a boat.

Here, the pleasure of the aerial view becomes more particular. It lets us see an arrangement holding even where its subject is missing. The vacancy belongs to the pattern.

But at the bottom of the frame, small figures interrupt the rows and rectangles. The image offers a diagram, then leaves people in it.

![Rows of boats and empty marina slips above a broad dark channel; two small figures stand on a pier at the bottom of the frame.](https://fieldlight.com/writing/at-two-heights/assets/berths-1600.webp)

*03 / Between arrivals — The empty slips. The figures on the pier.*

## III. The address

### Then the ground *asks something of you.*

![Worn red lettering reading STOP THE GENOCIDE on concrete, with red shoes and the photographer's shadow entering the frame.](https://fieldlight.com/writing/at-two-heights/assets/appeal-1600.webp)

*04 / An appeal — Worn paint; present tense.*

![A chalk face looks up from the pavement beneath lettering; striped strawberry trousers and red shoes enter from the lower left.](https://fieldlight.com/writing/at-two-heights/assets/reader-1600.webp)

*05 / A face — Looking down. Being looked at.*

“Stop the genocide” is fading into concrete. A foot could cover a word. The photograph stops long enough for the appeal to remain readable.

This writing does not sort traffic or reserve a berth. It asks someone who is passing to become someone who responds.

Nearby in the sequence, a chalk face looks up. The photographer’s clothes and shoes stay in the image. There is no clean division between the thing being noticed and the person doing the noticing.

*The reader is in the picture.*

## IV. The return

### Still *here.*

It would be easy to end with the city spread out beneath us. This photograph will not let the person disappear.

![A small person casts a long shadow inside a painted FIRE LANE. A metal fence and its striped shadow cross NO PARKING lettering; dark water lies beyond.](https://fieldlight.com/writing/at-two-heights/assets/fire-lane-1600.webp)

*06 / Above, again — A person. A shadow. A place to keep clear.*

*Fire lane. No parking.* From above, the words are still there. A fence throws a second fence across the ground. Between the markings, a person casts a shadow much longer than their body.

At the beginning, the photographer’s shoes made the body impossible to overlook. Here, looking for the body becomes part of reading the photograph.

The wider view is beautiful. It is also a view in which a person can become small enough to miss.

The camera can leave the body. *The picture keeps finding one.*

## About the sequence

Six photographs from two collections dated 20 September 2025: street photography and aerial photography. The sequence follows visual echoes rather than a route to retrace. All six frames are shown whole; select a photograph to look closer.
