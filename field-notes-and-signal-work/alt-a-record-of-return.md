# alt: a record of return

*An interactive record of recurrence, recognition, and the music carried across twelve years.*

`alt` is a playlist of songs I chose to keep. I did not arrange it as a sequence. With one or two exceptions caused by songs disappearing and being added back, its exported order approximates the order in which the songs became favorites.

That makes the list something other than a ranking. It is a rough chronology of recognition.

The visualization places all 202 songs along that exported favorite sequence. Each mark extends in two directions:

- **Above the line:** recurrence, measured by the play count reported in the Apple Music export.
- **Below the line:** time carried, measured from the date the song entered the music library to the date of export.

The result is not a portrait of taste assembled from genres or recommendations. It is a record of return: which songs stayed, which songs accumulated repetition, and which older entries continued to remain active long after they first entered the library.

## What the record holds

- 202 playlist entries
- 23,997 recorded plays
- library-entry dates spanning 2014–2026
- a median of 100.5 recorded plays per song
- 102 songs with at least 100 recorded plays
- 52.3% of all recorded plays distributed across the 50 most-played songs

The distribution matters. Attention is concentrated, but not captured by only a handful of tracks. The playlist contains a broad field of recurrence.

## What the record does not claim

The exported order is not a deliberately arranged listening order. It is used here as an approximate favorite sequence because the playlist was built through favoriting. One or two songs were dropped and later restored, so their current positions are chronology exceptions.

Apple Music's `Date Added` field records when a track entered the music library, not when it was favorited. Play counts are the counts Apple Music reported at the time of export. The source cannot establish every listening context, emotional meaning, or exact favorite date.

Those limits are part of the record. The system supplies measurements. It does not possess the interpretation.

## Public data and privacy boundary

The original XML remains private because it contains local file paths, persistent identifiers, library identifiers, and other machine-specific metadata.

The [public dataset](../assets/alt-a-record-of-return/alt-sanitized.json) contains only the fields used to construct the reading surface: exported sequence, title, artist, album, genre, release year, library-entry date, last-played date, play count, skip count, duration, and favorite status.

The platform counted. I decide what the count means.
