# Worked Example — From Notebook Photographs to a Bounded Finding

Version: 1.0  
Example type: real workflow, content redacted  
Purpose: show the trail without publishing the private source

## 1. Acquisition boundary

A creator recovered a storage-unit batch containing nine bound notebooks, notebook-associated loose material, and two standalone printed leaves. The creator photographed notebook covers, opening and closing pages, and body pages that stood out during handling.

The resulting Apple Photos album contained 526 photographs.

The accession boundary was defined as:

- included: the 526 photographs in the frozen album cutoff and the physical source objects they documented;
- excluded: later photographs, unphotographed notebook pages, born-digital counterparts not yet accessioned, and other storage-unit material;
- privacy default: private, with item-level restriction for family, health, credential, contact, or precise-location material.

The boundary prevented the photographed selection from being described as a complete digitization of all notebook contents.

## 2. Preservation

The unmodified export produced:

- 526 HEIC still-image masters;
- 525 Live Photo MOV companions;
- 526 XMP metadata sidecars;
- 1,577 preserved files in total.

Full-resolution JPEGs were created as working derivatives. Separate SHA-256 manifests verified masters and derivatives. OCR, cropping, and analysis were prohibited on the preservation masters.

## 3. Source and representation inventory

Each physical notebook received one stable source ID. Each photograph received a capture ID pointing to its notebook, loose leaf, envelope, or batch-level source.

For the notebook used in this example:

```text
Source: ARC-NB-0008
Representations: ARC-CAP-#### through ARC-CAP-####
Direct notebook photographs: 207
Coverage: substantial selected-page run, not complete notebook digitization
```

The source-versus-representation distinction allowed one notebook to remain one source while every evidentiary photograph stayed individually locatable.

## 4. Orientation before analysis

The notebook had the largest capture run in the batch. Its orientation map recorded:

- physical identity and date basis;
- capture range and coverage limit;
- visible internal sections and inserted material;
- classes of writing and research;
- named work references;
- high-risk pages requiring restricted handling;
- standout regions suitable for later packets;
- missing, unphotographed, or ambiguous portions.

The orientation did **not** claim that the notebook represented the creator's entire archive or that its densest pages were the archive's central meaning.

## 5. First scope correction

Because the notebook was unusually dense, it became the pilot for deeper work. That created a methodological risk: its content and structure could quietly define the other eight notebooks.

The correction was to stop deepening the pilot and complete a breadth-first orientation map for every notebook in the accession. Only after all nine were navigable could the dense notebook return as a bounded analytical source.

This produced a general rule:

> A useful pilot tests the method. It does not stand in for the population.

## 6. Bounded evidence packet

One packet examined the relationship among:

1. a handwritten notebook page;
2. a related born-digital file identified by the creator;
3. documentary metadata and a separate provenance note;
4. later records that used or interpreted the material.

The content remains private. The packet structure was:

| Included record | Reason |
|---|---|
| Notebook capture | Earliest preserved physical representation in the packet |
| Born-digital file | Candidate same-work or derivative relationship |
| File metadata | Documentary evidence for the surviving file state |
| Creator context | Identification and account not available from the artifact alone |
| Later reference | Evidence of subsequent use or interpretation |

The packet excluded unrelated pages from the same notebook and thematically similar materials that lacked a direct relationship.

## 7. Claim-level connections

The analysis did not create one large statement that the materials “were connected.” It separated claims:

| Claim | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| The photograph depicts the identified notebook page. | Direct visual evidence | High |
| The digital file is the item the creator associates with that page. | Creator supplied + direct comparison | High for identification |
| The surviving digital file state postdates the notebook representation. | Documentary metadata | Medium to High, depending on date basis |
| The notebook page caused the later file or event. | Inference | Not established |
| Similar symbolism validates an external factual claim. | Inference | Rejected without independent evidence |

The separation made it possible to preserve a meaningful relationship without converting chronology or symbolism into unsupported causality.

## 8. Analysis and counterevidence

The bounded synthesis stated:

- what the artifact order supported;
- what only the creator could identify;
- what metadata could and could not date;
- which similarities were direct textual or visual matches;
- which claims remained interpretation;
- what missing earlier versions could change the sequence.

The analysis retained negative evidence. Absence from one representation constrained claims about that representation but did not prove the element never existed elsewhere.

## 9. Second scope failure and retraction

Later, a whole-body synthesis relied too heavily on the newly cataloged historical notebooks and writing packages while omitting substantial current technical and institutional work that was already accessible.

The response was not to defend the synthesis. It was to record a scope-failure memo, retract the whole-body conclusion, inspect the omitted domains, and add a restart rule:

> No whole-corpus finding may be drawn from one accession family or conventional product category. It must declare its cross-domain boundary and test current work, historical sources, counterevidence, and creator recognition.

This correction is part of the method because analytical scope is an archival variable, not merely a writing choice.

## 10. Public derivative

This worked example publishes:

- source and representation counts;
- workflow stages;
- correction logic;
- evidence categories;
- the shape of the packet;
- methodological findings.

It does not publish:

- notebook images or quotations;
- family or health context;
- credentials, contact details, or precise locations;
- private interpretations;
- filenames that expose private content;
- the creator's unpublished work.

The result demonstrates a central capability of the method: private evidence can support a public methodology without the private evidence becoming public.
